The enduring conflicts between India and Pakistan have spawned countless narratives, alternately celebrated, romanticised and vilified on either side of the border, depending on the prevailing political climate. These stories are often fuelled by hostility and nationalist fervour, yet paradoxically accommodate moments of affection, shared sentiment and enduring human connection. For unimaginative screenwriters, few subjects offer such an apparently inexhaustible reservoir of dramatic possibilities as the fraught relationship between these neighbouring nations. Divided by history, politics and ideology, India and Pakistan nevertheless remain bound by a shared cultural inheritance, their destinies intertwined in ways that transcend the animosities separating them.
