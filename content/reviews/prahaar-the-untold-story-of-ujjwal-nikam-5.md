As a courtroom drama centred on the Indian lawyer who secured the sentencing of the one surviving terrorist behind the 26/11 Mumbai attacks, Prahaar: The Untold Story of Ujjwal Nikam is a predictably effective movie. There’s no dearth of storytelling around this tragedy, so the familiarity — the cultural fatigue — is now used as a weapon. Much like its central character, Prahaar postures, pauses, poses, argues, emotionally manipulates, yells, and wins. Given the subject, it’s hard to get wrong. The background score milks every moment; Lalit Prabhakar plays Ajmal Kasab as a brown-faced monster slowly disintegrating into a man; the CST shooting is recalled and filmed through the eyes of a child; the trauma is amplified so that the fruits of justice taste sweeter. And of course, there’s the patriotic closing argument.
