With endless resources and predestined eyeballs, it’s hard to go wrong with mythology as revered as the Ramayana. But it’s harder to get right, if there is such a thing. This double-edged sword is on full display in Nitesh Tiwari’s 195-minute film. The most remarkable aspect of Valmiki’s ancient epic, like all timeless poetry and prose, is that you don’t have to be religious to feel its influence. Nearly every tale ever told can be traced back to the works of Valmiki and Homer; it’s the origin story of storytelling itself. Given that the Ramayana was an introduction to literature for many of us — through illustrated pages, bedtime reading or television serials — the nostalgia does half the job. Not unlike comic-book fans who finally get to enjoy their favourite superheroes come alive on a big screen, we let anticipation define our viewing experience.
