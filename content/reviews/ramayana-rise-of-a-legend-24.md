Ramayana—the massive mythological epic starring Ranbir Kapoor, Yash, and Sai Pallavi—delivers on visual effects but suffers from a soulless, beat-for-beat execution. The film operates less as a bold cinematic adventure and more as a sanitised spectacle akin to Jon Favreau’s live-action The Lion King remake. We discuss the cultural and political weight surrounding the project—drawing parallels to Dhurandhar—and argue that a fear of offending audiences leaves director Nitesh Tiwari and co-writer Shridhar Raghavan with zero room to reinterpret the text. Consequently, Ram is written as a “vanilla” pushover with no flaws, while Sita is saddled with regressive, patriarchal costume tropes. Ironically, the film only comes alive in Lanka. We praise the humanised, modern characterisation of Ravana—especially the clever psychological reimagining of his ten heads—alongside engaging turns from Rakul Preet Singh as Shurpanakha and Vivek Oberoi as Vidyutjeev.
