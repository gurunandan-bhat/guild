The first half of Nayyi Navelli plays out like the answer to the question: What if TVF Gullak is pushed into the supernatural space? There’s a quirky Meerut-based family. An ancestral home. Lots of squabbling. Plenty of fondness parading as noise and taunts. And an hour-long emergency meeting on the terrace (against an artificial background of the city that puts the hand-painted sunsets from the 1990s to shame). The black sheep, Bantu, is desperately trying to convince the rest that the newest entrant in the family is a ‘daayan’ (witch); that his elder brother Raja’s wife, Mohini, is not a normal woman. It’s like watching a low-budget courtroom tale. Bantu painstakingly offers proof of the last few months and the mysterious circumstances of her arrival. He recalls weird scenes, narrates eye-witness accounts, and provokes the irritated family members to punch holes in his narrative. All this while an inauspicious and allegedly possessed Mohini — who will soon get multiple entry shots — is locked away in a room.
