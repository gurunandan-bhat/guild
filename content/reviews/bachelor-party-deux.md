Nearly two decades after his debut, Big B (2007), Amal Neerad remains perhaps our most cinephilic filmmaker. Few directors wear their movie love as openly, and Bachelor Party D’eux is no exception. The references just keep coming; the Fort Kochi mansion at the centre of the film is called Xanadu, borrowing its name from Charles Foster Kane’s palatial home in Citizen Kane. The interval’s big money shot sees the five protagonists locked in an elaborate Mexican standoff straight out of Reservoir Dogs. Then there’s the principal villain, who is almost always nursing a glass of milk instead of the customary scotch. A nod to the sinister Alex from A Clockwork Orange, you might think. Not quite. The film points us instead towards the Coens’ The Big Lebowski, where milk is the drink of choice. And just in case you were waiting for Kubrick, another character turns up in an A Clockwork Orange T-shirt. With Amal Neerad, name-dropping the masters is never too far away.
