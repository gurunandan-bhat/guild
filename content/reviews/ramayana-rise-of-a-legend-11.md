Like me, if you are of a certain vintage, then Sunday mornings in the '80s would carry one defining image — the family huddled together in front of the television, taking in every bit, beat and burst of Ramanand Sagar's Ramayan. Modern lore stands testament to the fact that streets went empty and shops remained closed on 78 successive Sundays, with Ramayan notching a mammoth 82 per cent viewership that included a whopping 650 million overall eyeballs. If we have to put the numbers in context, the eight seasons of Game of Thrones — a globally-acclaimed small screen-big scale TV experience — have a total audience which is tangibly lesser, even with the advantage of being available for posterity, enabling it to be seen by fresh and repeat audiences alike even as you read this. A rerun of Ramayan aired during the Covid-19 lockdown brought in a wave of nostalgia, but also curious new viewers, with a particular episode reportedly garnering 77 million views alone. For many, it was a throwback to a time when simple storytelling and emotional resonance reigned supreme. For us, Ramayan was not a part of childhood; it was our childhood.
