Long before the code of machines, there breathed a timeless rhythm, a song of righteousness written by a sage in the heart of India. Nitesh Tiwari’s ambitious two-part adaptation, Ramayana, is a cinematic leap of faith, seeking to bridge the infinite horizon of modern technology with the quiet, unyielding weight of human devotion. It feels ironic that a classic story meant to teach us the destruction of ego (ahamkara) is being delivered via a medium that demands we bow down to the vanity of technological scale. The Ramayana is fundamentally a story of subtraction. It is about a prince who willingly gives up a throne, walks barefoot into the wilderness, strips himself of material comfort, and defeats a scholar king whose fatal flaw was his insatiable obsession with his own grandeur, which hollowed out his intellect. By filtering this narrative through a modern global cinematic lens, the DNEG-backed version fundamentally reverses those values, replacing spiritual subtraction with digital multiplication.
