There is a wonderfully absurd film trapped inside Udta Teer. It revolves around Bilal Ahmed, a Pakistani spy embedded in India’s intelligence system as Vikrant Thapar. Bilal loses his memory in an accident and wakes up believing himself to be a loyal Indian patriot. The joke, and for a while a very effective one, is that he unknowingly begins dismantling the very terror network he once served. The film’s first half embraces this premise with an infectious willingness to be silly. Ayushmann Khurrana plays the amnesiac spy with conviction, while the late Mukul Dev steals scenes as a jihadi juggling terrorism and matrimonial aspirations. Suhail Nayyar is equally entertaining as a fellow Pakistani operative exasperated by his colleague’s sudden change of loyalties. He functions as the audience’s surrogate, repeatedly reminding us of the delicious irony at the heart of the story: a Pakistani sleeper network in India is being brought down not by intelligence brilliance, but by a bout of accidental patriotism.
