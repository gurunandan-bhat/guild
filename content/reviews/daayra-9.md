At what point does justice spill over into becoming revenge? When does "due process of law" degenerate into meaning legal loopholes to be taken advantage of by the perpetrator? How do law enforcers take the law into their own hands, aided by the footnote that the "law has to do its duty"? How does one decide what is within the "daayra" of the system and what isn't? These are just a few of the multiple questions — many uncomfortable, most for which no one can really have an emphatic answer — that Meghna Gulzar throws at the viewer in her latest film. Daayra, which translates to "limit", explores what the law really means in a country where most don't have the patience for the deliverance of justice, living as we are within a system that delays — and often denies — justice. Which side of the divide are we on? More importantly, can we — and should we — pick a side?
