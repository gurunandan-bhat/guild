t’s no surprise that Nitesh Tiwari’s Ramayana (also known as ‘Namit Malhotra’s Ramayana’, if you’re interested in monstrously insecure territory-marking) lives entirely in service of its bigness and digital shininess. But perhaps the most unfortunate thing about Tiwari’s sprawling, synthetic epic is that it isn’t all that exciting to talk about. Even bad films, middling films, noble misfires and ugly cash grabs offer something to hold onto and engage with. But when the act of discussion itself feels unexciting, it’s never a good sign. There’s little here that surprises or stands out, let alone thrills or delights.
