Given Hindi cinema’s obsession with hypermasculinity and patriotism, I’m surprised it took so long for a spy comedy to emerge. Actually, I’m not so surprised; imagine offending 99 percent of the country by cajoling them into joking about national security. Humour is such a risk. Irreverence can be such a crime (ask our standup comics). But it’s only fitting that a Kunal — an affable, witty, unpretentious and naturally unserious observer of Indian living — leads the charge. After giving Dil Chahta Hai the dysfunctional-buddy treatment in Madgaon Express, Kunal Kemmu directs himself in Vibe, a slacker comedy that’s shamed into an identity crisis. It’s the cinematic equivalent of watching two gloriously apolitical dudes accidentally pass a joint at a smoky house party to a proud WhatApp uncle; nobody is prepared for the night (and fight) that follows.
