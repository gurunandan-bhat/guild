Avinash Arun’s Prahaar, being marketed as “the untold story of Ujjwal Nikam”, might not be the most accurate description of the film. And that’s because it has little first-hand insight on the much-chronicled public prosecutor in the 1993 Bombay bomb blasts case. This is consistent with the Hindi film biopic, where the subject rarely has to work hard to become the protagonist. It’s something that is consistent here as well, where Rajkummar Rao (playing Nikam) gives a speech at a celebration, after the verdict of the 1993 bomb blasts case. “The foot soldiers faced the consequences, but the main conspirators are still free,” he says, “The job is not finished” and an inspirational score fills the background. I braced myself for yet another hagiography, where the subject is next to God. However, soon enough, Arun’s film zooms out from Nikam, introducing supporting characters, who, reluctantly, express the film’s larger ambitions.
