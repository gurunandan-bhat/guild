In theory, The Revolutionaries has a lot going for it. The sprawling historical drama tells the lesser-known story of the armed revolutionaries of India’s Independence movement: the underground rebels who fought the British with bullets and bombs before Mahatma Gandhi’s non-violent crusades, and the freedom-fighting ‘ancestors’ who set the stage for future folk-heroes like Bhagat Singh, Udham Singh and Chandra Shekhar Azad. Its primary antagonist is Lieutenant Colonel Dyer, before he became the world’s most hated Englishman for his role in the 1919 Jallianwala Bagh massacre. It’s the natural companion piece to the two seasons of Freedom At Midnight, also created and directed by Nikkhil Advani, who’s one of the more accomplished long-form filmmakers going around; he knows a thing or two about scale and period pieces. And it has a young cast, the kind that draws a ready-made Gen-Z fanbase to a ‘serious’ chapter.
