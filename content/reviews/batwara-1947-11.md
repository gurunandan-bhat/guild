Every nation carries within it a wound that history refuses to let heal. Few events in the history of the subcontinent have left a wound as profound, intimate and enduring as the Partition of British India in 1947. More than 15 million people were uprooted from their ancestral homes, while the death toll is estimated to have reached as high as two million. But statistics, however staggering, can never adequately convey the human cost of Partition—the homes abandoned overnight, families torn apart, identities violently rearranged, and memories passed from one generation to the next like an inherited grief. Batwara 1947 attempts to enter that vast historical tragedy through the intimacy of one household.
