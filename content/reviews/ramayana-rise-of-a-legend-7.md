Ever since Namit Malhotra announced his decision to make Ramayana, there has been widespread curiosity, speculation and discussion around the film. Puritans felt that there was no need to tamper with Hindu mythology, and cynics were sure it would go the Adipurush way and thus spoil it. Others weren't sure of the casting choices, and some were plain curious about how director Nitesh Tiwari would translate one of the greatest Indian stories for the big screen. Happy to report that Ramayana Part 1 delivers to a great extent. And no, it doesn't go the Adipurush way- if you know what I mean. For Indians, Ramayana has been a timeless story that has been passed down through generations. It's a great story that highlights the triumph of good over evil.Coupled with warfare, Ramayana has been and will always be for all seasons. Adapting the epic comes with a huge responsibility, especially in a country like India, which considers the epic sacred. And the makers of Ramayana: Part 1 take utmost care to present the core story as it is without taking much artistic liberty.
