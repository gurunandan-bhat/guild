A movie about the making of a movie is an old movie schtick which can be a hit or miss. But to make it with such heart and craft makes I Play Rocky a clear winner. The film is, of course, Rocky, starring Sylvester Stallone In and As Rocky Balboa, the loser who kept fighting, rocking back with each stab, refusing to go down. By the time I saw the film, many years had elapsed from the original release, and its hero had made a name for himself in doing similar kinds of roles, not to speak of fronting a mega-successful franchise, an endless fount which kept giving, all the way till its fifth iteration. Everyone loves a good underdog story, and Rocky’s is the ultimate: he even has to sell his beloved puppy because he has no money to keep him well fed. That’s one of the many moments in I Play Rocky, deftly directed by Peter Farrelly (Dumb And Dumber, There’s Something About Mary) that you are made to feel for young wannabe actor Micky Stallone (Anthony Ippolito) who is struggling to make something of himself in the New York of the 70s.
