A fundamental problem of Kanika Dhillon’s writing — and that is particularly evident in the case of the films in which there is a raging female protagonist — is that the ideas burst with immense possibilities, but the execution (and that stems from the root of its weak writing itself) is far from effective. Dhillon focuses on flawed, unapologetic women thrust into circumstances which often place them in a do-or-die decision-making pivot, but the result is more of an unhinged chaotic mess than a carefully-built complex quagmire. This is especially true of Kangana Ranaut’s Judgementall Hai Kya, in which Dhillon explored gaslighting, but the film’s convoluted look at mental health came in for much criticism. Or for that matter, Do Patti, with Kriti Sanon featuring in a double role, that felt like a superficial, glossy take on domestic abuse.
