Truth is what we ostensibly fight for, yet it is often the first casualty of the very battles waged in its name. Nowhere is this paradox more acutely felt than in a courtroom, where the clamour for retribution must yield to the rigours of evidence and the dispassionate demands of the law. It is this uneasy intersection of public outrage, legal obligation and the pursuit of justice that lends Prahaar: The Untold Story of Ujjwal Nikam its most compelling premise. Unfortunately, a film that understands the moral stakes of its subject does not always possess the dramatic finesse to do it justice. Directed by Avinash Arun and produced by Dinesh Vijan under the Maddock Films banner, Prahaar traces the professional journey of public prosecutor Ujjwal Nikam through some of India’s most consequential criminal proceedings. Revisiting the legal battles arising from the 1993 Bombay bombings and the 2008 Mumbai terror attacks, the film eventually settles on the trial of Ajmal Kasab, the lone surviving terrorist captured during the 26/11 carnage. Rather than merely reconstruct the horrors of that night, it turns its gaze towards the protracted judicial proceedings that followed, examining what it means to uphold the rule of law when public sentiment clamours for swift and uncompromising vengeance.
