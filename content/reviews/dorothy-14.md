In Dorothy—the new Tamil film written and directed by Karthik Subbaraj—what begins as a familiar rural gangster story seamlessly shifts genres into a sensitive exploration of a transgender woman embracing her true identity. We discuss how the film deliberately contrasts the suffocating, patriarchal toxicity of its village setting in the first half with a liberating, nature-filled journey in the second. We praise Subbaraj for defying the harmful “bury your gays” trope, allowing the protagonist to find a hopeful and positive resolution rather than facing the usual cinematic tragedy. However, we critique the film’s heavy-handed and preachy dialogue, particularly during a pivotal mountaintop scene where the characters feel more like directorial mouthpieces delivering social commentary than lifelong friends expressing their genuine feelings.
