If Indian literature ever had an all-time blockbuster, The Ramayana would sit right at the top. It might not have The Mahabharata’s endless moral tangles, but its characters, conflicts, and internal politics are still wide open to reimagining. The epic no longer belongs to Valmiki alone; it belongs to anyone who brings their own ideas, ethics, and worldview to the table. That feels like the exact spirit behind the disclaimer in Nitesh Tiwari’s Ramayana: Part 1. The filmmakers clearly know the minefield they are walking into. We are living through a hyper-sensitive, creatively cautious moment where the internet outrage engine will latch onto everything, from Ranbir Kapoor’s eating habits and Sai Pallavi’s political stance to A. R. Rahman’s faith. Just getting artists of this caliber to pool their instincts on a project built on a staggering, multi-crore budget feels like an act of defiance in itself.
