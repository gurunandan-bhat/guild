Indian streaming turns ten this year, and _Akka_ is what Netflix has to show for it. The platform introduced itself to the country with Anurag Kashyap and Vikramaditya Motwane’s S_acred Games_, a series with a worldview and the nerve to be bleak. Now we get a Netflix show set in Pernuru, a fictional kingdom ruled by women where an outsider arrives and threatens the peace. It could have been a chance to rethink the gangster genre. But _Akka_ is happy to imagine a female version of it, with the same swagger and the same rituals of loyalty and betrayal, only the pronouns changed, leaving the genre otherwise untouched. That  complacency says everything about the moment. Creators and platforms alike  treat streaming as an afterthought. Netflix still hasn’t cracked the country,  which has no single media appetite and no single disposable income, so its  shows keep falling back on the grammar of television: the loud score, the lots  of mutual glaring between characters, the plot that explains itself through  expository dialogue, and a narrative content to drag itself to oblivion.
