Much before the cinematic spectacle ‘Ramayana’ unfurls on silver screens, we get its partial retelling, a mini version inspired by the epic battle between the righteous and the sinful. We have nothing against films dipped in religious fervour, but when a film tries to be many things at one go, we can’t quite figure out whether ‘Nayyi Navelli’ is a social film masquerading as horror.com or mumbo-jumbo folklore being peddled as a divine subject. From the word go, the film wears its social conscience on its sleeve. A sprightly Dadima (Sushmita Mukherjee) tells her newly-wedded daughter Pinky (Mita Vashisht) that there is no rush to have children; your body, your choice. And all these woke ideas have come to her from Mohini. Now, who is Mohini? The beautiful Yami Gautam Dhar. Only, in the film, her devar Bantu (Anurag Thakur) is a doubting Thomas and takes hours to convince us, and his family, including her husband Raja (Adinath Kothare), how Mohini is no mortal but, god forbid, a daayan.
