In times when actors are obsessed with playing characters against type, Parivarik ManuRanjan reminds us how comforting it is when performers lean into their natural essence. Pankaj Tripathi effortlessly embodies a gentle, unassuming everyman, while Aditi Rao Hydari brings an innate serenity and grace. Together, they guarantee a film filled with Lucknow’s muted charm. Ranjan (Tripathi) is single at 40. Having spent his youth getting his three sisters married, he now lives alone in a crumbling mansion his father left him. He works in the electricity department, yet his own life has little light. His nosy colleague, Rajaram (Brijendra Kala is also the co-writer), is keen on finding him a match, though nothing sparks. Exhausted by the search, they place a newspaper advertisement for a housekeeper.
